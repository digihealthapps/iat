Research Context

Understanding Implicit Racial Preferences

The Race Implicit Association Test (IAT) measures reaction-time differences when associating White vs. Black faces with positive or negative concepts. Research published by Project Implicit indicates a significant ~30% reduction in implicit race bias over 2007–2025, alongside a steeper reduction in explicit anti-Black preference.

Long-Term Implicit Shift

Implicit racial attitudes have shifted steadily toward neutral over the last 18 years. Unlike many psychological traits once thought fixed, implicit preferences show persistent macro-level change.

2

Explicit vs. Implicit Divergence

Explicit self-reports show a much faster decline (-55.8%) compared to implicit measurement (-32.6%), highlighting differences between conscious attitude expression and automatic associations.

3

Generational & Regional Variance

Younger age cohorts and higher formal education levels correlate with lower mean D-scores. State-level aggregate data reveals notable regional variations across the US.

Methodology & D-Score Interpretation

What is the IAT D-Score?

The D-score is the standard metric derived from response latencies in millisecond pairings. It ranges roughly from -2.0 to +2.0:

  • D > 0.65: Strong automatic preference for European American vs. African American.
  • 0.35 < D ≤ 0.65: Moderate automatic preference for European American.
  • 0.15 < D ≤ 0.35: Slight automatic preference for European American.
  • -0.15 ≤ D ≤ 0.15: Little to no automatic preference (Neutral).
  • D < -0.15: Automatic preference for African American (slight, moderate and strong use the same cut-offs).

Data Sample & Limitations

Data comes from self-selected web participants taking the Race IAT at Project Implicit. While non-randomized, research by Charlesworth & Banaji demonstrates that these demographic and temporal trends closely mirror probability-sampled national panels.
